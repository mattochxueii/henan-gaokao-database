## 第四部分　写作　第一节（15分）

### 66.（15分）

答案：Open response

State all three positions and give comparative reasons in the open-day context. The wording allows any coherent order; the sample prioritises intervention where maps can fail.

6分　Rank all three options clearly and explain the relative order

5分　Ground reasons in the needs of new students at the event

4分　Use coherent, concise language suited to a school newspaper

### 范文

Making Open Day Easier to Navigate

I would put student guides at busy crossings first. A map can show the usual route, but a guide can respond when a corridor is crowded or a door has been closed. A map at the gate comes second: it gives everyone a shared point of reference, and some visitors will prefer to find their own way. A quiet room ranks third. It would help people with longer questions, but signs and guides must first make it possible for those visitors to reach the room.

## 第二节（25分）

### 67.（25分）

答案：Open response

Paragraph one must follow the act of taking out the bus, using it to explain a route that diverges from the model. Paragraph two must account for the volunteer returning with the sign and use that event to resolve the siblings' tension. The sample repairs access, keeps Nina and Ben active, and echoes the bus and sign without making the streetlights irrelevant.

7分　Continue from both required openings and preserve characters, premises and point of view

8分　Build a causal sequence from the unusable route to a feasible sign solution

6分　Develop the relationship through credible details and resolve the scene

4分　Language is clear, cohesive and near the requested length

### 范文

Nina asked Ben to take the bus out of his bag. They set it by the model's old entrance. When Ben moved it along the shortest road, Nina blocked the way with a folded ticket. “That's the door that doesn't work today.” Ben turned the bus, traced the longer route, and stopped it beside the working entrance. Nina asked a visitor to explain the route back to them. The visitor got it right, so they carried the ticket and the bus over to the volunteer.

At the end of the afternoon, the volunteer came over with the sign. She had moved the original one beyond the open door and made a second arrow from spare card. This sign, she explained, was for the model: the arrow could stay with the bus. Nina switched on the streetlights once more. Ben reached for the toy, then left it beside the new arrow. “It can wait here,” he said.
